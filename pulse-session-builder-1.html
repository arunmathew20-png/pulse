Stop the set, sit down, and call for help

  • Chest pressure, tightness, or the ache you remember
  • Pain radiating to jaw, neck, left arm, or between the shoulder blades
  • Breathlessness out of proportion to the effort
  • Light-headedness, greying vision, or a cold sweat
  • Palpitations, a racing pulse that will not settle, or a skipping rhythm
  • Unusual nausea or indigestion during exertion

Do not walk it off. Sit, stop, and use your emergency plan. One aborted session costs nothing.

The seven rules this app never breaks

  1. Breathe out on the effort. No breath-holding under load. A held breath spikes blood pressure and drops venous return in one move — the single worst habit for a post-infarct lifter.
  2. Two reps always left in the tank. Sets stop at RPE 7–8. Nothing here goes to failure.
  3. No one-rep maxes, ever. Strength is proven by adding reps and small plates, not by testing singles.
  4. Rest is prescription, not weakness. 90–120 s on compounds lets the pressure–rate product fall before the next set.
  5. Never skip the cool-down. Stopping dead after loading is when rhythm trouble likes to appear. Five minutes of easy movement, every time.
  6. Isometric holds stay short. Planks and wall sits capped at 45 s, breathing throughout.
  7. Effort is judged by feel, not the watch. If you are on a beta blocker your heart rate is capped artificially — RPE and the talk test are the honest signals.

Deliberately not in your library

These are not banned forever. They are excluded because the risk-to-return maths is poor for a well-recovered post-AWMI lifter who already gets his conditioning from running and swimming.

Pulse is a training tool, not medical advice. It assumes you have a current exercise prescription from your cardiologist and a normal recent stress test. Any new symptom outranks any plan in this app.
